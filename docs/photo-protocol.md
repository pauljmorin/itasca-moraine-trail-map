# Photo Location Check — a 5-Minute Test Before Your First Outing

**Why:** Our trail map is built from photos. Each photo quietly saves
*where* it was taken. If your phone saves the location, your photo
becomes a pin on the map — a kiosk, a sign, a parking spot — with no
extra work for you.

**This test makes sure your phone saves the location.**
Do it once, at home, before your first outing.

---

## Step 1 — Take one test photo

1. Step outside your home.
2. Take **one photo** of your mailbox or your driveway.
3. That's it. One photo.

## Step 2 — Send it to the shared folder

1. Open the shared photo folder.
2. Open **your named subfolder** inside it (the one with your name).
3. Add the test photo there.

**Important:** Always add the photo to the folder itself.
Never send it by text message. Texting removes the location.

## Step 3 — Wait for your green light

We check every test photo automatically.

- **Green light** — your phone saves locations. You are ready.
- **Red light** — no location was saved. Don't worry. It is a common
  setting, and it takes about a minute to fix. Use the card below
  for your kind of phone, then take a new test photo.

**Please wait for the green light before your first outing.**
Photos without locations cannot go on the map.

---

## Fix-It Card A — iPhone

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Privacy & Security**.
3. Tap **Location Services**. Make sure it is **ON** at the top.
4. Scroll down and tap **Camera**.
5. Choose **While Using the App**.
6. Turn **Precise Location** ON.
7. Take a new test photo and add it to your folder.

*To check a photo yourself:* open the photo in the Photos app and
swipe up. If you see a place name or a small map, the location was
saved.

## Fix-It Card B — Android Phone

1. Open the **Camera** app.
2. Tap the **Settings** gear inside the camera.
3. Find **Location tags** and turn it **ON**.
4. Also make sure the phone's main Location is on:
   swipe down from the top of the screen and tap **Location** so it
   is lit up.
5. If the camera asks "save location?" — tap **Yes**.
6. Take a new test photo and add it to your folder.

**Google Pixel phones — two extra checks** (Pixel is strict about this):

1. Open **Settings** → **Apps** → **Camera** → **Permissions** →
   **Location**. Choose **Allow only while using the app**, and turn
   **Use precise location** ON.
2. Take test photos **outdoors**, and give the phone a few seconds
   outdoors first. A phone that has not found its position yet saves
   the photo with no location at all. Opening Google Maps once, until
   the blue dot settles on you, wakes the location up.

*(Menu names vary a little between Android phones. If you get stuck,
call the coordinator — it is a two-minute fix together.)*

---

## The Standing Rules

1. **Originals go to the folder.** Never by text message — texting
   strips the location off the photo.
2. **Use your named subfolder**, so we know whose photos they are.
3. **If the camera ever asks about location, say yes.**
4. Out on the trail, just take the photo. One clear photo of each
   thing — sign, post, kiosk, parking area — standing close enough
   to read it.

Thank you — every photo you take becomes part of the chapter's map.
