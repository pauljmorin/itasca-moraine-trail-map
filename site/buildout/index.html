The North Country Trail in Minnesota: what is built, what is still road and bike path, and what is proposed.

106.1 miles

Mesabi Trail — read this first

The Mesabi Trail is not shown here as complete. The Minnesota Lottery said in October 2026 that it is “officially complete,” but LCCMR Proposal 2027-341 requests $1,650,000 to build the final 2.5 miles, West Two River to Tower, by June 30, 2029. The Mesabi Trail’s own October 13, 2026 update says 0.5 mile is not finished. To confirm with the St. Louis & Lake Counties Regional Railroad Authority.

The NCTA official line still routes hikers on MN-135, Embarrass to Tower. The as-built trail has replaced most of that road walk; the map shows the official line, not the as-built trail.

Planning — readiness

McCarthy Beach to Hwy 169: route mapped, appears viable, needs ground-truthing; many parcels in the Conservation Fund land exchange; needs a new “Triple Divide” chapter; Superior NEPA environmental assessment possibly 2027, realistically 2028–29; state environmental assessment worksheet (EAW) also needed.

Hwy 169 to Bear Head State Park: Howard Wagoner Trail Club to affiliate 2026/27; Hwy 169 to Quick Bridge digitally mapped and deconflicted from the Prospector ATV trail, needs ground-truthing; Quick Bridge to McKinley Park via the Tower Municipal Forest Plan and a St. Louis County lease update; $266,000 Recreational Trails Program grant received; Vermilion State Park — about 1.5 mi of new construction would complete the alignment, but state park permission is the barrier; Vermilion to Bear Head 75% ground-truthed.

Bear Head to Glipi Road: Bear Head State Park is about 90% existing trail, with a new piece needed on the east side; Bear Head to Ely explored, mapped and ground-truthed, for the next Superior NEPA round; Ely to Glipi needs mapping, Garden Lake bridge question, Minnesota Power; Glipi to Snowbank: about 12 miles close to ready to build, awaiting the Fernberg environmental assessment decision and a state EAW.

EAW in review: 23.79 miles (10.8 Lake County, 12.78 Itasca County, 0.21 Cass County) — the working model for the compliance process.

Planning — risks, as the chapters named them

Motorized / ATV conflict is the single greatest risk. The NCT is reportedly not at the table in regional trail-deconfliction meetings while Mesabi, ATV, ski and snowmobile interests are.

No single finalized master plan for the Arrowhead corridor to present to partners.

Grant dependency: the pipeline depends on LCCMR / ENRTF cycles; LCCMR money starts about 18 months after a proposal.

Permissions still to win: Vermilion State Park, School Trust easements, Section 106 cultural surveys and SHPO concurrence, NEPA and state EAW per segment.

15 miles of the Zone 4 Option A route (Cavour’s Route) lies outside the approved planning corridor — needs NPS sign-off.

Planning — motorized competition (ATV & snowmobile)

About 1,059.5 miles of designated ATV trail in the four corridor counties (St. Louis 465, Itasca 401.5, Lake 114, Cook 79 — sums of published system mileages, not a surveyed network length), against 1.44 miles of off-road foot tread in the Arrowhead gap. The ATV network is the dominant recreational trail system in the corridor by mileage, it is still expanding, and it is backed by a dedicated funding account the NCT does not have.

What the map shows: ATV lines (purple) and snowmobile lines (blue) within 2 miles of the proposed Arrowhead line and the 2004 corridor, from the Minnesota DNR’s own trail services. GIS-measured in the corridor: 120.1 mi of ATV trail inside the 2004 corridor and 367.7 mi of snowmobile trail inside it. The proposed line runs within 100 m of motorized trail for 23.8 mi (ATV) and 56.8 mi (snowmobile), crossing ATV trail at 20 locations and snowmobile trail at 32 locations. The current official line already runs within 100 m of motorized trail for 14.0 mi (ATV) and 48.3 mi (snowmobile). Mileages are GIS-measured off the DNR services, Nov 13, 2026 — they will differ from published system totals, which are shown in the table below. Crossing locations cluster intersection points within 250 m: where the proposed line runs along a motorized corridor the two digitizations weave across each other, so raw point counts would overstate the crossings.

The headline overlap: the proposed line does not avoid the motorized system — it runs alongside it. 50.9 of its 101.4 miles lie within 100 m of the Taconite State Trail (the groomed snowmobile spine, Grand Rapids to Ely), and 16.6 miles lie within 100 m of the Taconite–Big Aspen ATV connection. The reroute would share the Taconite corridor for roughly half its length: easier land in one sense, direct competition for the same tread in another.

The Highway 2 rule, stated honestly: north of U.S. Highway 2, unsigned forest access routes in managed state forests are open to OHV riding unless posted closed (Minn. Stat. § 84.777) — and user-created routes get used with little consequence. That is a rule about riding unsigned routes, not a right to build a trail: building a new trail needs DNR designation and posting, and environmental review. HF 1012 / SF 1245 would end the north/south split (signed trails only statewide); neither is enacted. Most county roads in St. Louis County are also open to ATVs, so the road walk hikers use today is largely rideable already.

Snowmobiles are a different competition: they do not rut tread the way wheeled vehicles do, but they need wide groomed corridors — 20–26 ft for a shared segment, 32–36 ft at crossings — and groomers up to 17,000 lb. The 165-mile Taconite State Trail runs Grand Rapids to Ely, the same endpoints as the gap, as a groomed snowmobile spine. Named snowmobile systems total 598 miles.

The organizations: ATV Minnesota, 73 member clubs (est. 1983), funded through the ATV Dedicated Account ($60 per three-year registration plus unrefunded gas tax); MnUSA, 250+ clubs and 10,000+ members, funded through the snowmobile trails and enforcement account. IRRR granted $2.16 million to five ATV clubs in a single board meeting (May 2025), and LCCMR funded the Moose and Seven Beaver ATV upgrade — the same fund the NCT planning proposal targets.

The deconfliction-meeting claim — that the NCT is not at the table while Mesabi, ATV, ski and snowmobile interests are — is reported in the chapter’s folder but was not verified against any meeting record; it is not asserted here as fact.

Planning — ATV trails, Brainerd to Canada

1,630.5 miles of designated ATV trail from Brainerd to Canada (Crow Wing 186.5, Cass 166, Aitkin 89.5, Itasca 420.5, Blue Ox 74, Voyageur Country 36, St. Louis 465, Lake 114, Cook 79), against 1.44 miles of off-road foot tread in the Arrowhead gap. ATV-only the total is 1,599.5 miles — the 1,630.5 counts the DNR A-Z rows as published, including three that are not ATV: Miller Hills 14 (OHM-only), Alger Grade 13 (OHM-only) and Mesabi Mountain 4 (ORV-only).

Read the total the way the research states it: 1,630.5 is an arithmetic sum of published system mileages, not a surveyed network length. Systems share tread in places (the Prospector Loop ties into the Lake and Cook systems; Taconite ATV segments are also snowmobile trail; the Blue Ox is also a snowmobile trail in winter), so the total contains some double-counted tread. GIS-measured off the DNR service, the systems drawn on this layer total 1,200.3 mi — the two figures differ because of segmentation and shared tread, and neither is silently substituted for the other.

Where the service geometry diverges most from the published figure: the DNR service measures Voyageur Country at 116.5 mi against 36 designated miles — the service appears to include signed connector routes beyond the designated trail. Stony Spur II measures 58.9 mi against 30 published — the DNR GeoPDF labels the Stony Spur II + Jackpot loop as 60 miles, so the service geometry is likely the combined loop. Emily-Outing measures 28.3 mi against 56 published, Crow Wing Southern Loop 43.6 against 60, Pine Center 13.5 against 23 — the service appears to carry part of those systems. Rabey Line + Hill City measures 35.1 against 19 published, Moose River Connector 16.7 against 7, Blue Ox 88.1 against 74, Soo Line North (Aitkin) 60.2 against 48. The published figure is used in the tables and popups throughout; the measured figures are shown here so the difference is visible, not hidden.

The corrected Arrowhead core is 1,059.5 miles (St. Louis 465, Itasca 401.5, Lake 114, Cook 79), not the ~1,083 the prior file carried: the prior file double-counted the Iron Range OHV State Recreation Area, carrying both a “main unit 36” row and a “West Unit 36” row. The DNR A-Z lists West Unit 36 and East Unit 13. The wider 1,630.5 = corrected core 1,059.5 + Crow Wing 186.5 + Cass 166 + Aitkin 89.5 + Blue Ox 74 + Voyageur Country 36 + Rabey Line 19.

What the map shows: this layer (purple, same color as the Arrowhead ATV layer) draws the designated systems of the wider corridor whole — Brainerd to the Canadian border — from the Minnesota DNR’s own OHV trail service. Systems already drawn on the Arrowhead layer are not drawn twice; the table marks them “Drawn (Arrowhead)”. Turn the layer on to see the full corridor; the default view is the two gaps, so the map zooms to the layer the first time it loads.

Three numbers that must not be added together: designated miles (the 1,630.5 above); club-system miles — what a club actually maintains and signs, including forest roads and county-road rights-of-way (Voyageur Country is ~200–250 miles of signed corridor routes against 36 designated miles; the Northwoods Regional ATV Trail Alliance claims 500+ miles, which overlaps the Soo Line corridor figures in the table below); and everything legally rideable — the DNR’s January 2025 reply to the Minnesota Chapter of The Wildlife Society totalled 101,417 miles statewide, including unsigned forest-access routes. That last figure is a different universe from designated trail and is not used here.

The north end: the Blue Ox Trail (74 mi, Northome to Littlefork) is the only designated ATV trail that physically connects the Itasca systems to the Koochiching / Voyageur Country systems. Everything north of it toward Canada is proposed, not built: Voyageur Country Phase 2 (up to 125 mi total — 39 existing route + 78 existing with improvements + 8 new trail; DNR record of decision April 11, 2025, negative, no EIS ordered; permitting stage) and the Blue Ox Connection (about 4.6 mi, Ericsburg to the Blue Ox; LCCMR 2027 proposal, not funded). The closest designated access toward Canada today is Sheep Ranch Road, about 40 miles south of International Falls.

The organizations: ATV Minnesota (ATVAM), 73 member clubs, est. 1983, funded through the ATV Dedicated Account; the Northeastern Regional ATV Joint Powers Board (St. Louis, Koochiching and Lake Counties, organized August 2021) coordinates the northeastern systems and is currently working to extend the trail from Kabetogama toward Ray and on to International Falls and connect it to the Blue Ox. A University of Minnesota Extension study (August 2024) estimated 219,230 riders used ATV trails in Koochiching, Lake and St. Louis Counties in 2023, contributing $36.1 million in economic activity. ATVAM supports SF 593 / SF 594 (2025, funding from the ATV Dedicated Account) and opposes HF 1012 / SF 1245, which would end the north/south split in the Highway 2 rule; neither is enacted.
