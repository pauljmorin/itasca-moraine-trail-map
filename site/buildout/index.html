Finishing the Trail

The North Country Trail in Minnesota: what is built, what is still road and bike path, and what is proposed.

For the congressional delegation: the case on one screen.

Arrowhead gap

182.6 miles

1.44 mi of foot tread — 0.8% off-road

Grand Rapids to Ely and on to Snowbank Lake. The rest is Mesabi Trail bike path and road.

Western gap

106.1 miles

5.98 mi of foot tread — 5.6% off-road

North Dakota border (Fort Abercrombie) to east of Detroit Lakes.

Authorized by Congress, March 2019 (Dingell Act, P.L. 116-9).

Still on road and bike path six years later.

Minnesota will pay for the planning — $643,850, zero federal cost — the Park Service has not yet accepted it. Proposal 2027-179, latest copy labeled “Not Submitted Final,” March 14, 2026; not submitted.

Planning — readiness

Route mapped, McCarthy Beach to Snowbank. Ground-truthing, NEPA and state EAW still needed per segment. Full notes: docs in the repo.

Planning — funding status

Mesabi final 2.5 mi: $1,650,000 requested (LCCMR 2027-341, not an award). Planning proposal 2027-179: $643,850 requested, not submitted. Nothing awarded.

Planning — risks, as the chapters named them

Motorized / ATV conflict is the single greatest risk. The NCT is reportedly not at the table in regional trail-deconfliction meetings.

No single finalized master plan for the Arrowhead corridor to present to partners.

Planning — motorized competition (ATV & snowmobile)

About 1,059.5 miles of designated ATV trail in the four corridor counties (St. Louis 465, Itasca 401.5, Lake 114, Cook 79 — sums of published system mileages, not a surveyed network length), against 1.44 miles of off-road foot tread in the Arrowhead gap.

What the map shows: ATV lines (purple) and snowmobile lines (blue) within 2 miles of the proposed Arrowhead line and the 2004 corridor, from the Minnesota DNR’s own trail services. GIS-measured in the corridor: 120.1 mi of ATV trail inside the 2004 corridor and 367.7 mi of snowmobile trail inside it.

The headline overlap: 50.9 of its 101.4 miles lie within 100 m of the Taconite State Trail (the groomed snowmobile spine, Grand Rapids to Ely). The reroute would share the Taconite corridor for roughly half its length.

North of U.S. Highway 2, unsigned forest access routes are open to OHV riding unless posted closed (Minn. Stat. § 84.777) — but building a new trail still needs DNR designation and environmental review. HF 1012 / SF 1245 would end the north/south split; neither is enacted. Snowmobile groomers run to 17,000 lb.

Planning — ATV trails, Brainerd to Canada

1,630.5 miles of designated ATV trail from Brainerd to Canada (Crow Wing 186.5, Cass 166, Aitkin 89.5, Itasca 420.5, Blue Ox 74, Voyageur Country 36, St. Louis 465, Lake 114, Cook 79), against 1.44 miles of off-road foot tread in the Arrowhead gap. ATV-only the total is 1,599.5 miles. Sums of published system mileages, not a surveyed network length.

The corrected Arrowhead core is 1,059.5 miles, not the ~1,083 the prior file carried — the prior file double-counted the Iron Range OHV State Recreation Area. Don’t add the counts: Voyageur Country is ~250 mi of signed routes against 36 designated miles; the Northwoods Alliance claims 500+ miles, overlapping the Soo Line figures.

What the map shows: this layer draws the designated systems of the wider corridor whole — Brainerd to the Canadian border — from the Minnesota DNR’s own OHV trail service. Systems already drawn on the Arrowhead layer are not drawn twice.

The north end: the Blue Ox Trail (74 mi) is the only designated ATV trail connecting the Itasca systems to the Voyageur Country systems. Everything north toward Canada is proposed, not built: Voyageur Country Phase 2 (up to 125 mi) and the Blue Ox Connection (about 4.6 mi).
