Data sources

  • Trail line: North Country Trail Association, on behalf of the National Park Service (official public depiction)
  • Parcels: Minnesota Geospatial Commons (MnGeo) compiled county parcels; Hubbard County open data (Hubbard is not in the state compilation). The parcels layer is off when the map opens; its data loads only when you switch it on.
  • Non-road access: Minnesota DNR — state park trails & roads, OHV trails, state trails, snowmobile trails, hunter walking trails
  • Roads: Minnesota DNR / MnDOT road centerlines, plus Minnesota DNR state forest roads
  • Roads and access lines are shown within a corridor of the trail; faded ends continue beyond it.
  • Mile markers: North Country Trail Association Minnesota half-mile points (the NCTA mileage index)
  • Mile markers show the NCTA reckoning; the chapter tracker's mile figures differ by roughly 46–48 miles and are labelled as tracker miles where shown.
  • Sections & gaps: the chapter's adoption tracker and segment boundary points, joined onto the official line
  • Waypoints (parking, camping, shelters, water, points of interest) and the Find index: North Country Trail Association public point data, plus the layers above
  • Trail alerts: North Country Trail Association trail alerts service — alert points pulled Nov 11, 2026, shown with their own dates and wording; the layer is off when the map opens and loads only when you switch it on
  • Leech Lake Reservation: U.S. Census Bureau, TIGER/Line American Indian Areas (BAS 2026 vintage; GEOID 1940R). The layer is off when the map opens and loads only when you switch it on. The boundary is the federal reservation boundary as published — not a legal survey. 2.28 mi of the depicted trail (Chip-12) lies inside the reservation.
  • Map sheets: North Country Trail Association Minnesota map series (edition of March 16, 2026) — sheets MN-023 to MN-044 under the NCTA map sheets layer, and the official sheet named on each section's card. The layer is off when the map opens and loads only when you switch it on; sheet images appear at closer zooms, with the sheet outlines and numbers as a locator below that.
  • Chapter places: the Itasca Moraine Chapter's own records, maintained by the chapter — seeded from North Country Trail Association point data in November 2026 (the campsites) and confirmed or corrected by the chapter since; where a chapter record exists it replaces the NCTA point. The layer is off when the map opens and loads only when you switch it on
  • Base maps — Topographic: OpenTopoMap (the default; CC-BY-SA; map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, SRTM); USGS Topo 1:24k (USGS The National Map); National Geographic (Esri)
  • Base map — MN LiDAR Hillshade: Minnesota Geospatial Image Service (MnGeo / Minnesota DNR), derived from 1 m LiDAR. The terrain view — the moraine itself; recommended for reading the land.
  • Tree height (overlay) — MN LiDAR canopy height and hillshade (LiDAR): built from the state's 2022 second-generation LiDAR (MnGeo / Minnesota DNR) — a shaded view of the treetops, and canopy height as colour (white at ground level, deep green at full height, so clearcuts show). White is open ground; 2 m is head height; 20 m is the old-growth line (height is a guide, not proof of old growth). The overlay runs along the trail corridor only and ends at its edge; the two canopy bases it replaces were retired in November 2026.
  • Base maps — Aerial: Aerial (NAIP 2023/25) (USGS The National Map); Esri World Imagery; Aerial + Topo (NAIP 2023/25) (NAIP imagery with topo labels); Aerial (2014) — Esri World Imagery Wayback, the release of 30 December 2014: the aerial view as it stood at the end of 2014
  • Base maps — Streets & trails: Streets © OpenStreetMap contributors; Trails by CyclOSM (© OpenStreetMap contributors); Winter (snow trails) — OpenSnowMap's ski and snow-trail lines over the MN LiDAR Hillshade

Adopted sections show the adopters' initials (for example "JR") — the chapter's real adopter names are kept in its working files and never appear on this map. Parcel owner names and addresses, as published in the county parcel records (public record), appear in parcel popups when the parcels layer is switched on.
