About this map

Itasca Moraine Chapter of the North Country Trail Association — trail map

What this map is for

This is the working map for the Itasca Moraine Chapter of the North Country Trail Association — the volunteers who look after about 100 miles of the North Country National Scenic Trail in northern Minnesota, across Hubbard, Cass, Becker and Clearwater counties. It is built for three jobs at once: running the chapter (which stretches are adopted, where the gaps are), coordinating the volunteers who maintain the trail, and showing the trail to the public. Right now the map is set to its working view — the chapter’s own view, for getting work done.

How it is built

The map is a plain website — pages, a map, and data files in open formats — so it can be handed to the chapter and kept going without any special software. The trail itself comes from the North Country Trail Association’s own published map service, the official depiction of the route. On top of that the chapter’s own records are drawn: which sections are adopted and by whom, the places along the trail (campsites, parking, water, shelters), and the things the volunteers photograph and look after (signs, posts, boardwalks, bridges, bollards, and the rest), each with its own number. Land information — timber sales, forest health, land ownership, conservation easements — comes from the counties, the state, and the Forest Service. The full list of every dataset, and where it comes from, is in the Data sources document.

The map is hosted on GitHub Pages, and the chapter’s own data lives in the same place, in files anyone can read. The volunteers classify their own photos through a simple web page, and a small service lets them update a pin’s classification straight from the map — no one else’s judgment is involved.

Privacy

The map shows only what the chapter has chosen to make public. Volunteers who adopt a stretch of trail are shown by initials only — names, phone numbers and email addresses never appear on the map. Land parcels show the owner’s name because that is published county record; tax and assessment details are left out.

Who is behind it

The map is built and maintained by Paul Morin, a chapter volunteer, for the Itasca Moraine Chapter. It is the chapter’s map — branded as the chapter, not as the national association — and it is built so the chapter can take it over and run it.
