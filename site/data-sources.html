Data sources

Itasca Moraine Chapter of the North Country Trail Association — trail map

Every dataset this map draws on, what it is, and where it comes from. The map shows the Itasca Moraine chapter’s stretch of the North Country National Scenic Trail — 76 miles across Hubbard, Cass, Becker and Clearwater counties in northern Minnesota — plus the wider Minnesota trail for context. Layers that are off when the map opens load only when you switch them on; nothing here is downloaded into the map except the chapter’s own data.

A note on freshness. Most of these layers are snapshots, pulled on the dates given. A timber sale, a forest-health survey or a trail alert is a record of its year, not a live feed — the year on each layer is the freshness signal. Where a source updates on a schedule, the schedule is noted.

The trail

The official depiction of the North Country National Scenic Trail through the chapter’s extent: route and centreline features with status, surface, ownership and chapter attributes. 84 features, clipped to the chapter’s working extent. Mileage on this map comes from the chapter’s own segment pins, not from any outside published total: 73 miles adopted, 76 miles chained into one continuous line (78 miles stored in the official file), with a 3-mile unadopted gap.

The same official line, kept for the whole state instead of the chapter extent: 348 features, 566 miles, from the North Dakota border at Fargo to the Border Route Trail junction at Grand Portage. North Country Trail — Star of the North

The Star of the North chapter’s built extent, Minnesota 84 north of Longville to Highway 6 northeast of Remer: 12 features, 27 miles measured (26 miles of footpath plus 1 mile of on-road connectors), drawn as a chapter line like the Itasca Moraine line. The official line’s chapter attribute tags only 2 of these 12 features as Star of the North (10 miles); the rest carry the Arrowhead tag, so reading the state line by its chapter attribute showed only about 10 miles of Star of the North tread. The extent here is the chapter’s own definition, not the attribute: the 12 official-line features between the two highway crossings, chained end to end (150-metre endpoint tolerance) and clipped at the west end. The Minnesota 84 crossing is placed at longitude −94.21, from the road’s northern terminus and Longville — a judgment call, good to about a third of a mile. The 4 miles of footpath west of that cut is Itasca Moraine territory by the chapter definition and is already drawn by the chapter line. The chapter’s “about 30 miles” counts the full 31-mile chain from the Itasca Moraine boundary; the clipped extent is 27 miles.

Source: North Country Trail Association, on behalf of the National Park Service — same nct_public FeatureServer. Built November 18, 2026 by scripts/build_stn_line.py; published November 19, 2026.

Road walk — where the trail follows public roads

Three toggles in the “Road walk” group, all off by default: the Road walk line itself (56 pieces), the Speed limits badges (one per western segment), and the Accident-prone areas markers (three) — 73 features in all. The western road walk is the on-road stretch of the North Country Trail west of the chapter — Wilkin, Otter Tail and Becker counties, 15 segments, 78 miles drawn — and the Arrowhead road pieces are the on-road pieces of the Grand Rapids-to-Ely gap, 14 pieces, 25 miles. The Mesabi Trail bike path is not road walk and is not drawn here; it has its own layer under bike trails.

The western segments. The 15 segments come from Trail Research Brief T07 — Western Road Walk: The Safety Case, Segment by Segment (November 15, 2026; data retrieved November 16, 2026): each segment is defined by its NCT mile range (MN-0.0 to MN-106.5) and its road name, taken from the NCTA Minnesota Map Series (MN-001 to MN-020). The line’s geometry is the NCTA-service gap line (site/buildout/data/gap-western.geojson, 90 features), ordered west to east by a graph walk from the Red River end (endpoint adjacency within 2 km) and clipped to T07’s segment ranges. The ordering was checked against T07’s mileposts — Fergus Falls lands at chain mile 39.5–42.4 against T07’s MN-40.0–43.5, Maplewood Church Road at 67.3 against MN-66.0–68.5, the Becker County line at 95.1–96.4 against MN-94.8–97.0 — and chain miles track T07 miles within about a mile throughout. The drawn western line measures 78.1 miles against the segment ranges’ 80.2: the 2.1-mile shortfall is the NCTA GIS running about 2% shorter than the map-series miles, not a missing stretch.

Speeds. Every speed shown is either the statutory limit — 55 mph rural, 30 mph urban, 65 mph expressway (Minn. Stat. § 169.14) — or the Otter Tail County Safety Action Plan’s “majority 55 mph” finding for county segments. The badge shows the limit at the badge’s spot: 55 mph everywhere except Seg 7 (Fergus Falls city streets, 30 mph) and Seg 14 (the US-10 crossing, 65 mph). Each badge sits at its segment’s midpoint by cumulative chain miles, except Seg 14, whose badge sits at the US-10 crossing (chain mile 95.2) because that is the segment’s expressway portion; its midpoint falls in Frazee, where the limit is 30 mph. Badge positions are approximate within about a mile, and the popups say so. Traffic counts (AADT) come from MnDOT’s TCDS stations, with the count year shown; Segs 3 and 8 have no TCDS station, and the popups say so rather than borrowing a neighbour’s count.

One badge was removed, not reconciled. Seg 12 (Vergas) has no speed badge. The Otter Tail County Board minutes of January 3, 2023 record a MnDOT speed study recommending no change in 40 mph in Vergas; the brief says 30 mph in Vergas. Paul ordered the badge removed on November 19, 2026 rather than reconciling the two figures, so the Seg 12 line popup carries no speed row and a rebuild does not resurrect the badge. The Seg 12 line itself and its crash marker stay.

Crash spots. The three markers are the Otter Tail County Safety Action Plan’s High-Injury Network corridors on or next to the western road walk (plan pp. 60–64), with their systemic-risk tiers (Table 10, pp. 73–75): CSAH 88 north of Fergus Falls, HIN 6.0, Tier 3; CSAH 4 in Vergas, HIN 6.0, Tier 3 (CSAH 41 in the same segment is Tier 1); CSAH 1 / Tower Road in Fergus Falls, HIN 7.0, Tier 3 — the highest score on the western route. Each marker sits at its corridor’s chain mile (39.5, 85.5 and 56.5 respectively), approximate within about a mile, and each popup carries the plan’s corridor extent verbatim. The caveat in every crash popup is part of the record, not boilerplate: in Otter Tail County in 2020–2024 there were 17 pedestrian crashes (1 fatal or serious, 16 non-severe) and 2 bicycle crashes on the county highway system, and no published record attributes any crash to a hiker — no hiker fatality or injury on the western road walk is documented. Segment-level pedestrian and bicycle counts for the Wilkin and Becker segments could not be resolved from public sources, and the layer does not claim them.

Shoulders: edge condition, not width. No as-built shoulder width is published for any western segment, so the layer carries no numeric shoulder width for any segment — the field is absent, not zero and not a design-table value. What it carries per segment is the edge condition from T07’s “Shoulder Width / Edge Condition” column: MnDOT’s TCDS omits shoulder widths in public station views, so the column reads “Not published in MnDOT TCDS” for every segment (Segs 3 and 8 have no TCDS station at all), and the layer copies that wording — the popup’s shoulder row is a direct copy of the T07 table cell, lightly compressed, with the plan’s systemic-risk tier and Edge Risk 2S/3 deficiencies kept where the cell carries them. The one wording change is Seg 7: the cell reads “Concrete sidewalk” and the row text adds concrete sidewalks along about 3.64 miles in NCTA GIS, rendered as “Concrete sidewalk along ~3.64 mi (NCTA GIS); no shoulder — walkers on sidewalk” — the “no shoulder” clause is a wording summary [judgment], the sidewalk being the documented edge condition for that segment. Four sources were checked and rejected as as-built widths: MN Rules 8820.9920 (design-table widths for new construction, not measurements); the MnDOT Bikeways map (its statewide service is blocked to text fetch, and its data standard carries no numeric shoulder-width field); MnDOT District 4’s shoulder-widening prioritization study (exists but could not be located — a request to District 4); and the county bikeways maps (per-road values would need a visual map read). The Federal Highway Administration’s guidance of a walkable shoulder at least 4 feet wide is a benchmark, not a measurement, and the map does not score segments against it. The Arrowhead road pieces carry no shoulder fields at all — nothing was found for them.

Observed speeds: none published. No per-segment observed speed exists in any source checked, so the layer carries no observed-speed field and the posted and statutory speeds stand alone. MnDOT’s Speed Monitoring Program (57 automated stations) reports average 85th-percentile speed aggregated by roadway category only, with no published station list, so no station can be tied to a road-walk segment — the category average must not be read as a segment speed. MnDOT speed studies do exist for CSAH 1, CSAH 1 in Weetown, CSAH 4 and 60 in Vergas, CSAH 35, CSAH 41 and CSAH 88 (Otter Tail County Board minutes, January 3, 2023), but only the recommendations are public, not the measured 85th-percentile speeds; the studies themselves are a records request to MnDOT District 4 or the Otter Tail County Highway Department. T07 contains no observed-speed data at all.

Endpoints and lengths. Every line feature carries its drawn length and the trail it leaves and joins at each end, added November 19, 2026. Lengths are measured from the drawn geometry and shown rounded to the nearest mile. Western endpoints are found by walking the ordered NCTA-service chain outward from the segment’s T07 mile range: a foot, paved or sidewalk feature reached within 1 chain-mile is named from its own name and kind (for example “Prairie Wetlands Learning Center (foot tread)”); if the walk runs along road into the next T07 segment, the end reads “Road walk continues — Seg N”; if it meets an untyped feature, or a mile passes with no tread and no next segment, the end reads “No adjoining trail identified in the map data”. The chain ends are special cases: Seg 1’s west end is the Red River, the western end of the route, and Seg 15’s east end runs past the end of the NCTA-service chain, so it is checked against the statewide NCT line, which continues east as footpath. Arrowhead piece ends take the nearest foot tread or paved multi-use path within 500 metres, by point-to-line distance, across the Arrowhead gap line’s foot and paved features and the bike-trails layer; nothing within 500 metres leaves the field blank and the popup falls back to “No adjoining trail identified”. No endpoint name is invented: a name comes only from an adjoining feature’s own name, or from the fixed fallback strings above.

What is not in this layer. The Arrowhead road pieces carry the line only — no speed badge, no crash marker, no shoulder fields — because the packet holds no crash analysis and no verified per-segment speeds for the Arrowhead road walk. The 16.3-mile Highway 6 road walk between Remer and Cohasset is not in this layer either: it is drawn dashed, as on-road, in the statewide NCT line, but it carries no speed, shoulder or crash facts in the Road walk group, and whether to add it there is still Paul’s decision. The Mesabi Trail bike path (about 115 miles, paved) is not road walk. Built by scripts/build_roadwalk.py; full method, judgment register and pull log in SOURCES.md in the map repository.

Wetlands (NWI) — trail routing

The National Wetlands Inventory along the whole Minnesota trail, classified for trail routing: 91,479 polygons in a 2-mile corridor around the statewide trail line, 321,583 acres. Each polygon carries a routing call — MAYBE (85,155 polygons, 181,967 acres) means a short crossing only, with the structure set by the water regime: seasonally flooded or saturated ground takes a bog bridge or boardwalk, semipermanently flooded ground a short narrow boardwalk, temporarily flooded or saturated ground a turnpike or puncheon; NO-GO (6,324 polygons, 139,616 acres) means avoid — open or permanently flooded water, large bog and muskeg complexes, and perennial riverine channels. There is no GO polygon in this layer: GO is the absence of a wetland polygon, and a soil alone can never make a wetland GO. The classification is the chapter’s synthesis — no published Cowardin-to-routing matrix exists — so every judgment in it is labeled as judgment in the popup and in SOURCES.md.

Reading the map: a dashed amber fill is MAYBE — short crossing only; a solid red fill is NO-GO — avoid. Every popup names the classification, the rule that fired (W3–W9, with the judgment tag where one applies), the Cowardin code and water regime in words, the wetland type, acres, county and region, and the structure the crossing would need. The same key sits under the “Wetlands (NWI)” toggle in the panel and in the map legend.

Screening only. The inventory is not a delineation: it identifies about 90% of wetlands and classifies about 75% correctly, 95% of features sit within 5.9 metres of their true position, and the minimum mapped feature is half an acre. A field delineation and the permits come before any MAYBE becomes a route. The layer does not check DNR public waters, calcareous fens, or rare natural communities — no public layer for those is in the viewer yet. The federal gate is Nationwide Permit 42 (½ acre or less, pre-construction notification required); Minnesota’s Wetland Conservation Act runs avoid, then minimize, then replace, with a de minimis exemption of 400 or 10,000 square feet depending on type and location — confirm with the local government unit.

Source: US Fish and Wildlife Service, National Wetlands Inventory — USA_Wetlands FeatureServer (Minnesota content is the DNR June 2019 update; per-polygon vintage is not published). Pulled November 18, 2026. Full method, rule tables, judgment register and pull log in SOURCES.md in the map repository. The Border Route Trail as carried in the USDA Forest Service National Forest System Trails service — 24 features, 46 GIS miles measured (the service’s miles field sums 45; the published length is ~65 mi). It shares tread with the North Country Trail along the Minnesota–Ontario border, from the Gunflint Trail area to the Pigeon River, where the Superior Hiking Trail’s northern terminus also sits. The Forest Service line and the NCT line are two surveys of the same tread and sit up to ~350 m apart in places. Bold red line (with the Superior Hiking Trail, the two Partner Trails), off by default.

Source: USDA Forest Service, National Forest System Trails service — EDW_TrailNFSPublish_01 MapServer. Public domain. Pulled November 16, 2026.

Driftless Hiking Trail

The built pieces of the Driftless Hiking Trail in Iowa County, Wisconsin — 4 features, 6 GIS miles: Phoebe Point Trail (1 mi), Welsh Hills Trail inner route (1 mi) and Knobs Road Trail (2 mi), all assembled from OpenStreetMap, plus the Welsh Hills outer loop / Ridge Section (2 mi, approximate), traced from the Driftless Area Land Conservancy’s Welsh Hills trifold map because OpenStreetMap carries only the inner route there. The conservancy’s published figures are higher (Phoebe Point ~1 mi, Ridge Section 3 mi, Knobs Road ~3 mi) — the traced and OSM lines are the ground geometry; the published figures likely include approaches and connectors not on the maps. The Weaver Road segment is built (~1–2 mi) but is in neither OpenStreetMap nor any published map, so it is not drawn. Built pieces only — NOT the full planned trail. No planned-route line is drawn: no published GIS geometry for the planned ~100-mile loop (Governor Dodge State Park to Blue Mound State Park, by way of the Lower Wisconsin River near Spring Green) exists, and the corridor is not definable as a line from the published descriptions. Bold green line (with the Border Route Trail and the Superior Hiking Trail, the three Partner Trails), off by default.

Source: OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL) — Phoebe Point Trail way 1248941083, Welsh Hills Trail way 1248943005, Knobs Road Trail way 1248953013, pulled November 16, 2026; Welsh Hills outer loop traced from the Driftless Area Land Conservancy’s Welsh Hills trifold map (DT-Trifold-Welsh-Hills-Segment), georeferenced to the OpenStreetMap segments (GCP residuals 64–157 m; traced lines approximate to about ±50 m typical, ±100 m worst case).
